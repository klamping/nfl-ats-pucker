# NFL Against-the-Spread Pick Rubric

Use this rubric to evaluate whether the spread appears accurate and whether either side has a meaningful edge. It is a research framework, not a guarantee of results.

## How to Score

For each factor, assign:

- `+2`: Strongly favors the selected side
- `+1`: Slightly favors the selected side
- `0`: Neutral, already reflected, or unclear
- `-1`: Slightly favors the other side
- `-2`: Strongly favors the other side

Record the reasoning, not just the number. A factor should not receive a positive score merely because it supports an initial opinion.

## 1. Market and Spread Context

Evaluate the number before evaluating the teams.

- Opening spread
- Current spread
- Expected closing spread
- Available alternate numbers
- Price or vig on each side
- Whether the line has crossed a key number such as 3 or 7
- Size and timing of line movement
- Whether movement followed a known injury, lineup, weather, or coaching update
- Whether movement appears to be based on public narrative or informed information
- Consensus across sportsbooks
- Whether the line is unusually different from comparable market numbers
- Whether the spread already appears to include the factor being studied

Questions:

- What was the original market expectation?
- What information caused the line to move?
- Is the current number better or worse than the available earlier number?
- Is the perceived edge based on the number, or only on liking one team?

## 2. Team Strength

Assess current ability rather than relying on record or reputation.

- Overall offensive quality
- Overall defensive quality
- Special-teams quality
- Opponent-adjusted performance
- Recent performance compared with season-long performance
- Quality of opponents faced
- Performance against strong and weak teams
- Point differential beyond wins and losses
- Efficiency beyond raw yardage and points
- Turnover luck
- Red-zone luck
- Defensive or offensive results likely to regress
- Coaching and scheme changes
- Stability of the team’s identity and role

Questions:

- Is the team actually improving or declining?
- Are recent results supported by repeatable performance?
- Is the team’s record better or worse than its underlying play?

## 3. Current Player and Roster Status

Estimate who will actually play and how much they will contribute.

- Starting quarterback availability and health
- Backup quarterback quality and experience
- Offensive-line availability and continuity
- Skill-position injuries and expected usage
- Defensive-front availability
- Cornerback and safety availability
- Linebacker availability and communication roles
- Kicker and punter status
- Suspensions and disciplinary absences
- Players returning from injury
- Players active but likely limited
- Expected snap share and workload
- Recent changes in depth-chart position
- Trade-deadline or midseason roster changes
- Loss of leadership or role-specific expertise

Questions:

- Is the market’s adjustment for an absent player reasonable?
- Is the replacement player better or worse than a generic replacement assumption?
- Does the absence affect a unit that is especially important in this matchup?

## 4. Player Development and Team Cohesion

Account for teams changing as players gain experience together or as problems develop.

- Quarterback and receiver familiarity
- Offensive-line continuity
- Defensive communication and continuity
- Number of new starters
- Young-player development
- New coaching or scheme installation
- Improvement or deterioration in execution
- Recent changes in player roles
- Evidence of effort, focus, or discipline changes
- Reports of internal or organizational turmoil
- Whether apparent improvement is supported by more than final scores

Treat claims about morale or quitting as uncertain unless there is measurable supporting evidence.

## 5. Matchup and Scheme

Determine whether the teams’ strengths interact in a way that the broad spread may not capture.

- Passing offense versus pass defense
- Rushing offense versus run defense
- Pass rush versus offensive-line protection
- Quarterback pressure response
- Receiver separation versus secondary coverage
- Tight ends and running backs against linebacker coverage
- Explosive-play offense versus explosive-play prevention
- Short-yardage and red-zone matchups
- Early-down success
- Third-down tendencies
- Turnover creation versus ball security
- Run-pass tendencies
- Defensive personnel against the opponent’s preferred formations
- Coaching tendencies and in-game adaptability
- Divisional familiarity
- Whether the matchup changes expected pace or scoring

Questions:

- Does one team possess a specific strength that directly attacks the opponent’s weakness?
- Is the matchup likely to make the spread more or less accurate than usual?

## 6. Schedule and Situational Factors

Consider the circumstances surrounding the game.

- Rest advantage or disadvantage
- Short week
- Bye week
- Consecutive road games
- Long-distance travel
- Time-zone changes
- Early kickoff for a traveling team
- Home-field advantage
- Divisional game
- Primetime game
- Rivalry or emotionally important game
- Lookahead game before a major opponent
- Letdown game after a major win
- Playoff or elimination pressure
- Final-week motivation
- Whether starters may be rested
- Schedule congestion

Do not assume that a narrative matters. Look for evidence that the situation affects performance or that the market systematically misprices it.

## 7. Game Environment

Evaluate conditions that affect expected scoring and team performance.

- Wind strength and direction
- Rain, snow, or extreme precipitation
- Temperature
- Humidity
- Dome, retractable-roof, or outdoor stadium
- Field surface
- Altitude
- Field condition
- Expected game pace
- Expected run-pass balance
- Whether weather affects one team more than the other

Wind and field conditions may matter more than temperature alone, especially for passing, kicking, and deep-ball offense.

## 8. Previous-Game Interpretation

Do not treat the final score as a complete description of the previous game.

- Final margin
- Margin before the game became effectively decided
- First point after the first quarter at which either team reached a 99% in-game win probability
- Score and largest lead when that 99% win-probability threshold was reached
- Scoring by each team before and after the 99% win-probability threshold
- Garbage-time points
- Whether late touchdowns made a blowout appear close
- Turnovers and defensive scores
- Performance before and after the game became one-sided
- Whether starters were rested late
- Injury timing
- Opponent quality
- Whether the final score is likely to influence public perception

Questions:

- Did the previous result reveal genuine strength or merely late-game scoring?
- Did either team reach a 99% win probability after the first quarter, and did later scoring occur after that point?
- How would the final margin and each team’s scoring look if points after the 99% win-probability threshold were treated as garbage time?
- Could the market overreact to the final score?
- Did the team play better or worse than the score suggests?

## 9. Public Perception and Narrative

Use this category cautiously. It is a source of possible market error, not automatic evidence.

- Recent nationally visible win or loss
- Popular or heavily followed team
- Star quarterback or coach
- Defending champion or highly hyped team
- Revenge narrative
- Media coverage and public sentiment
- Recent prime-time performance
- Betting popularity
- Overreaction to a single dramatic result
- Underreaction to an unremarkable but meaningful performance

The question is not whether a narrative exists. The question is whether it has caused the spread to move beyond the football evidence.

## 10. Statistical Reliability

Separate repeatable performance from noise.

- Sample size
- Recency of the data
- Stability of the metric
- Opponent adjustment
- Turnover variance
- Fumble recovery variance
- Red-zone touchdown rate
- Defensive interception rate
- Explosive-play variance
- Dependence on a few unusually large plays
- Whether performance occurred in garbage time
- Whether the metric is affected by game script
- Whether the statistic is already incorporated into the spread

Give more weight to stable efficiency and usage information than to small-sample totals.

## 11. Model and Evidence Quality

Before trusting a conclusion, check how it was formed.

- Was the information available before kickoff?
- Is the conclusion based on multiple seasons or a small sample?
- Does it hold across different opponents and seasons?
- Does it improve on a market-only baseline?
- Does it remain useful when the line changes?
- Is the evidence based on opening lines, closing lines, or both?
- Could the result be caused by selecting the pattern after seeing the data?
- Are similar factors being counted multiple times?
- Is the model reacting to the same information already reflected in another factor?
- Does the signal persist outside the data used to discover it?

## Confidence and Weekly Ranking

Rank games by confidence only after reviewing all games. The highest point value should go to the pick with the strongest combination of:

- Clear separation between estimated team performance and the spread
- Reliable evidence rather than a single narrative
- Stable roster and injury assumptions
- A matchup or situational factor not fully reflected in the market
- Fewer unresolved uncertainties

Do not assign the highest value merely because a team appears likely to win outright. The relevant question is whether it is likely to beat the spread.

## Red Flags

Avoid high-confidence picks when:

- The argument depends mostly on a recent final score
- The line moved and the reason is unknown
- The pick requires several uncertain players to perform as expected
- The perceived edge is already fully reflected in the spread
- The supporting trend comes from a small or highly selected sample
- The analysis relies on a public narrative without measurable support
- The team’s recent performance was dominated by turnovers or garbage time
- The pick is being made because the other side feels unpopular
- The conclusion changes only because of the desired point ranking
